Survodutide
the liver-first dual agonist

One molecule, two hormonal switches — GLP-1 for appetite, glucagon for burn — from Boehringer Ingelheim and Zealand Pharma. While rivals chase the scale, survodutide’s program leans into the liver: obesity trials plus a dedicated push in MASH, the fatty-liver disease with no approved playbook until recently. Still investigational everywhere.

Quick read

Survodutide (BI 456906) is a dual GLP-1/glucagon receptor agonist co-developed by Boehringer Ingelheim and Zealand Pharma. Its SYNCHRONIZE program tests it for obesity, while parallel trials target MASH (metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis) — where glucagon’s liver-fat effects are the point, not a side quest. Phase 2 reported strong weight loss and liver-fat reductions; phase 3 is underway. Not approved anywhere — trial-design facts only, not recommendations.

01The molecule

What survodutide actually is

Survodutide is an investigational dual GLP-1/glucagon agonist from Boehringer Ingelheim and Zealand Pharma — built for obesity and MASH (fatty-liver disease) together, with the liver as the strategic prize.

Same dual-agonist architecture as its rivals — but the program’s center of gravity is the liver, not just the scale.

GLP-1: the appetite switch

Activates the GLP-1 receptor — less hunger, slower stomach, steadier sugar. The proven, familiar half shared with the whole drug class.

Glucagon: the liver switch

Activates the glucagon receptor — raising energy burn and, crucially, directly reducing liver fat. For survodutide, this isn’t a bonus; it’s the strategy.

Two diseases, one molecule

Obesity via the SYNCHRONIZE program; MASH (fatty-liver disease) via dedicated liver trials. Few weight drugs aim at both — that’s the differentiator.

Plain English

If most weight drugs are scale-first, survodutide is liver-first: the glucagon half isn’t just extra burn, it’s a direct attack on liver fat — the organ damage hiding behind obesity. Same two switches as its rivals; a different destination.

Two keys, one molecule — the receptor combination is the identity.

Obesity — phase 3

The SYNCHRONIZE program; 2026 SYNCHRONIZE-1 read out at 76 weeks in adults without diabetes. Investigational — no approval.

Type 2 diabetes — in program

Being studied for glycemic control alongside weight. Separate trials, separate verdict — not a package deal.

MASH / MASLD — phase 3 liver data

Dedicated liver program: 2024 phase 2 MASH and 2026 phase 3 MASLD results. Strong phase 3 ≠ approval for every indication.

03Liver-first logic

Why the liver is the prize

MASH — fatty liver progressing toward scarring — had almost no approved options until recently. Survodutide’s glucagon half attacks liver fat directly, making the liver the strategic target, not a side effect.

Two receptor systems, one molecule — receptors stay on the membrane; effects play out in their own tissues.
GLP-1 and glucagon pull blood sugar in opposite directions — the balance, not the burn, is the therapy.
Lipid droplets shrink over weeks and months — never vanish overnight; fibrosis regression is a separate, slower question.
Why the liver matters

MASH starts as liver fat, progresses to inflammation and scarring (fibrosis), and can end in cirrhosis. Weight loss helps — but a drug that directly defats the liver aims deeper.

Glucagon’s home turf

The liver is glucagon’s main stage — it’s where the hormone naturally acts. A glucagon agonist for liver disease is fighting on friendly ground, biologically speaking.

The measurement challenge

Liver trials need biopsies or advanced imaging — slower, costlier, and harder than stepping on a scale. That’s why MASH programs move deliberately.

Plain English

The logic: eat less (GLP-1), burn more and defat the liver (glucagon). Most rivals stop at the scale; survodutide’s program keeps going to the biopsy slide. If the liver data hold, it’s not just a weight drug with a hobby — it’s a liver drug that also cuts weight.

06Head to head

Survodutide vs the field

The name-soup, decoded. Four drugs, four receptor fingerprints — and more receptors never automatically means stronger.

Four receptor fingerprints — the combo defines the drug, not the number of switches.

Survodutide vs tirzepatide

Tirzepatide pairs GLP-1 with GIP; survodutide pairs it with glucagon. One is an incretin story, the other is an energy-and-liver story — different biology, different data to watch.

Survodutide vs retatrutide — the close call

Both carry glucagon-receptor agonism, so they get confused constantly. The difference: retatrutide adds GIP on top (GLP-1+GIP+glucagon) while survodutide is GLP-1+glucagon only — different incretin activity, different profiles.

Why combos differ

GIP, GLP-1, and glucagon each pull different metabolic levers (appetite, insulin, energy burn, liver fat). A different combo means a different weight-and-metabolism fingerprint — not a ranking.

The key distinction

If you mix up survodutide and retatrutide, ask: where’s the GIP? Retatrutide has it; survodutide doesn’t. If you mix up survodutide and tirzepatide, ask: where’s the glucagon? Survodutide has it; tirzepatide doesn’t.

09Risks & unknowns

Risks & unknowns

A liver-first dual agonist carries the class risks plus glucagon-specific questions. Here’s what trials saw, what the mechanism suggests, and what’s still unmeasured.

Observed

Investigational only

Observed fact: survodutide is not approved anywhere. Anything sold under its name is unregulated — there is no legitimate supply outside trials.

Observed

Class-typical GI effects

Observed fact: trials reported nausea, vomiting, and gut complaints, dose-dependent — the familiar GLP-1 trade-off, present here too.

Theoretical

Glucagon’s heart-rate question

Theoretical: glucagon-axis drugs can nudge heart rate and cardiac workload. For a liver-and-weight drug taken for years, cardiovascular outcomes need dedicated proof — pending.

Theoretical

The glucose tightrope

Theoretical: glucagon raises blood sugar; GLP-1 is supposed to counteract it. In patients with fragile glucose control, the balance deserves watching.

Unknown

Long-term dual exposure

Unknown: years of combined GLP-1 + glucagon signaling is uncharted — trials measure months to a few years.

Unknown

Liver-specific unknowns

Unknown: rapid liver defatting sounds purely good — but how fast is safe, and what happens to scarred tissue long-term, is still being learned.

✅ What it does

  • Activates two receptor systems — GLP-1 (appetite, glucose) and glucagon (energy burn, liver fat).
  • Reduces appetite and food intake — the main driver of weight loss.
  • Reduces liver fat measured on imaging and biopsy readouts — gradually, over months.
  • Causes GI effects in a meaningful share of trial participants — mostly during dose escalation.

❌ What it doesn’t do

  • Not a fat-dissolving compound — it does not selectively destroy adipocytes.
  • Liver-fat reduction ≠ reversing cirrhosis — advanced scarring is a different question.
  • Doesn’t guarantee any individual outcome — trial averages are population data.
  • Doesn’t prove long-term heart safety — cardiovascular outcomes need dedicated trials.
GI effects: the honest picture

In the phase 2 MASH trial, nausea, diarrhea, and vomiting were more frequent with survodutide than placebo — a physiological consequence of the mechanism, often worst during dose escalation, not a scary anomaly.

Plain English

Known: investigational, with class-typical gut effects. Theoretical: heart-rate and glucose-balance questions from the glucagon half. Unknown: long-term dual exposure and the pace of liver remodeling. Strong phase 2 doesn’t close any of these boxes.

10Glossary

Understanding Survodutide Terminology

Twelve terms, each with the science and a plain-English translation. Tap any term to expand it.

The scienceBI 456906, an investigational dual agonist that activates both the GLP-1 receptor and the glucagon receptor. It was co-developed by Zealand Pharma and Boehringer Ingelheim and is being tested in phase 3 trials (the SYNCHRONIZE program) for chronic weight management, plus separate trials for MASH, a serious form of fatty-liver disease. It has not been approved by any regulator for any use.

Plain EnglishAn experimental drug that presses two buttons at once: one quiets hunger, the other tells the liver to burn fat. Two companies built it together, and it is now in large global trials — but it is not approved and no doctor can prescribe it yet. It is the liver program, more than the weight program, that makes this molecule stand out from its rivals.

The scienceGlucagon-like peptide-1, an incretin hormone released by intestinal L-cells after eating. It acts on receptors in the brain to reduce appetite, slows gastric emptying, and stimulates insulin release in a glucose-dependent manner. Synthetic GLP-1 receptor agonists are the approved drug class behind the modern weight-loss medicines, and they form the foundation survodutide builds on.

Plain EnglishA natural gut hormone released after you eat that tells your brain you are full, slows how fast your stomach empties, and helps steady blood sugar. Drug versions of it are the famous weight-loss medicines — survodutide’s GLP-1 half is borrowing that proven trick. It is the best-understood half of the molecule, with years of approved medicines behind it.

The scienceA peptide hormone secreted by pancreatic alpha cells whose classical role is raising blood glucose by stimulating glycogen breakdown and glucose production in the liver. It also increases energy expenditure and promotes hepatic fat oxidation, which is why drug developers are pairing glucagon-receptor activity with GLP-1 in obesity and liver-disease programs. Unopposed glucagon would raise blood sugar too much, so pairing it with GLP-1’s glucose-stabilizing action is the design rationale.

Plain EnglishThe counterpart to insulin: when blood sugar runs low, it tells the liver to release stored sugar. At drug doses it also burns extra energy and helps the liver clear out fat. On its own it would push blood sugar too high — that is why it is always paired with the GLP-1 half, which keeps sugar steady.

The scienceMetabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis, the inflammatory, progressive stage of metabolic fatty-liver disease. It begins with fat accumulation in liver cells plus inflammation and cell injury, and it can advance to fibrosis, cirrhosis, and liver cancer. For years it had no approved drug therapy; the first conditional approvals only arrived in 2024, and several agents are now in late-stage trials.

Plain EnglishA serious form of fatty-liver disease where stored liver fat is joined by inflammation and cell damage. Left unchecked it can scar the liver into cirrhosis or lead to cancer. Until very recently, doctors had no approved drugs for it at all — which is why survodutide’s liver program is such a big deal.

The scienceThe phase 3 clinical-trial program testing survodutide for chronic weight management, sponsored by Boehringer Ingelheim across multiple large global studies. Phase 3 is the final, large-scale stage of testing — randomized and placebo-controlled — intended to confirm efficacy and safety before a drug can be submitted to regulators for approval. The 76-week SYNCHRONIZE-1 results were reported in 2025.

Plain EnglishThe name of survodutide’s giant final-round trial program for weight loss — thousands of participants, mostly compared against a placebo group, run around the world. Phase 3 is the last and largest testing stage before a drug can be submitted for approval. These are the studies that decide whether regulators will ever approve the drug.

The scienceThe abnormal accumulation of triglyceride fat droplets inside liver cells, defining the earliest stage of fatty-liver disease. Simple steatosis often causes no symptoms and can be reversible, but it is the starting point of the chain that leads to inflammation and scarring. MRI-based liver-fat measurements in survodutide’s MASH trials track steatosis reduction directly.

Plain EnglishA fancy word for a fatty liver — fat stored inside liver cells where it does not belong. On its own it is often harmless and reversible, but it is the first step on the road toward inflammation and scarring. Unlike scarring, simple steatosis can often be reversed if the underlying cause is addressed.

The scienceThe formation of excess scar tissue in the liver in response to chronic injury or inflammation, as stellate cells deposit collagen and the organ gradually stiffens. Fibrosis stage is the strongest predictor of liver-related death in MASH. Halting or reversing fibrosis is the key outcome that MASH drug trials ultimately aim to demonstrate.

Plain EnglishScar tissue forming inside the liver after long-term injury or inflammation. A fatty liver can bounce back; a scarred liver cannot, at least not easily. Slowing or reversing scarring is the real goal of every MASH drug trial.

The scienceA single molecule engineered to bind and activate two different receptors. Survodutide is a dual agonist at the GLP-1 receptor and the glucagon receptor, combining appetite suppression and blood-sugar control with increased energy expenditure and liver-fat reduction. The strategy aims for a greater metabolic effect than either pathway alone.

Plain EnglishOne drug that flips two switches at the same time. In survodutide’s case: the hunger-and-sugar switch (GLP-1) plus the energy-burn-and-liver switch (glucagon). The bet is that two effects together beat either one alone.

The scienceA Danish biotechnology company founded in 1998 that specializes in peptide therapeutics. It invented the peptide that became survodutide (BI 456906) and partnered with Boehringer Ingelheim to develop it through large-scale clinical trials. In the partnership, Zealand supplied the peptide science while Boehringer supplies the trial infrastructure.

Plain EnglishThe Danish biotech that designed the survodutide molecule. These are the peptide specialists who did the original science. The giant trials that followed are run by their partner, Boehringer Ingelheim.

The scienceA large German pharmaceutical company founded in 1885, active across human and animal health. It is co-developing survodutide with Zealand Pharma and sponsors the global SYNCHRONIZE phase 3 program. Within the collaboration, Boehringer leads clinical development, regulatory strategy, and any future commercialization.

Plain EnglishThe big German drug company running survodutide’s development. It provides the funding, the global trial network, and the regulatory muscle. Think of Zealand as the inventor and Boehringer as the machine that tests the invention.

The scienceA drug that is still being tested in clinical trials and has not received marketing authorization from regulators such as the FDA or EMA. Investigational drugs cannot be prescribed or sold outside of clinical-trial settings and approved research protocols. Survodutide carries this status for both of its programs, obesity and MASH.

Plain EnglishStill in testing — no regulator has approved it, no doctor can prescribe it, and you cannot get it at a pharmacy. “Investigational” is the official label for “not finished proving itself yet.”

The scienceAn inactive treatment given to a control group in a clinical trial, identical in appearance to the real drug but containing no active ingredient. Comparing outcomes against the placebo group isolates the drug’s true effect from expectation, lifestyle changes, and the natural course of disease. Every survodutide efficacy claim comes from a survodutide-vs-placebo comparison.

Plain EnglishThe fake-drug group that every real result gets measured against. Some participants get the drug, others get a lookalike with nothing in it — and the difference between the two groups is the drug’s actual effect. It keeps expectations and wishful thinking from polluting the results.
